What I think design is for.

Short enough to read standing up. Specific enough that you could disagree with me, which is the point — a manifesto nobody can argue with isn't one.

One

The boring systems deserve the best designers. Anything with an audit log attached is load-bearing for someone's actual job.

Two

Most "user education" problems are unfinished design. If it needs a tooltip to be usable, the tooltip is a receipt for something unresolved.

Three

Design the failure state first. The happy path will get attention from everyone; the half-completed, timed-out, partially-permissioned state will only ever get it from you.

Four

Whimsy is not the opposite of rigour. A system can be exacting and still have a sense of humour about itself.

Ask me in a year. I reserve the right to have changed my mind, and I'd rather date my opinions than pretend they're permanent.
