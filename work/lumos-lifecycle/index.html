Employee lifecycle, end to end

Provisioning that fires the moment someone shows up in the HR system, and deprovisioning that finishes instead of stalling halfway.

— Time to first access
— Manual tickets removed
— Orphaned accounts

Context

Lumos connects HR systems, identity providers, and the long tail of SaaS apps a company actually runs on. Lifecycle management is the promise that all three stay in agreement about who works here and what they can touch.

The people configuring this are IT and security admins who are measured on things going quietly right, and who inherit the blame when they don't.

The problem

Joiner–mover–leaver breaks in three predictable places. New hires wait days for the access they were supposed to start with. Internal moves go unmanaged, so permissions accumulate and nothing is ever taken away. Departures leave accounts alive in systems nobody remembered were connected.

The uncomfortable version: most companies don't have a provisioning problem. They have a deprovisioning problem, and they find out during an audit.

Role & constraints

I owned the end-to-end design: the workflow model, the configuration surface, and how state is communicated once a workflow is live. I worked with [PM] and [engineers] over [duration].

What I didn't control: the underlying connector coverage, and the shape of the data arriving from each customer's HRIS — which varied more than any design could fully absorb.

Process

Modelling the workflow

The first model treated lifecycle as a set of rules per app. It fell apart as soon as a customer needed the same app granted differently by team, title, and location.

Getting timing right

Access has a shape in time: granted slightly before day one, revoked at exactly the right moment on the way out, with a grace window where a manager can intervene. Most of the design work was about making that timing visible and adjustable without turning it into a scheduling app.

The solution

Attributes from the HR system drive everything. An admin describes the rule once — this team, this title, this location, gets this access — and the workflow runs on every joiner, mover, and leaver from then on.

Outcome

A fintech customer now drives provisioning across their entire app stack from Workday attributes, with no manual step in between.

I wrote about the thinking behind this in more depth for the Lumos blog: The Future of Lifecycle Management Is Unified, Intelligent, and Human.

What I'd change

I'd push harder on the failure states. The happy path got the attention it deserved; the design for a workflow that half-ran, against a system that timed out, arrived later than it should have.
